# Generative AI Use Principles

Using Generative AI requires critical judgment, as the output tends to converge toward an average level. Real experience must always guide the process.

## Details

<!-- source:berkeley-haas-resume-standards start -->
### From Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md
- GenAI feeling "brilliant" on a first draft usually just means you were inexperienced at that specific task — not a signal the output is actually great. ([[raw/Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md#12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails|Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md › 12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails]])
- GenAI (LLMs), by construction, converges to an *average* (or below-average) output. ([[raw/Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md#12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails|Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md › 12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails]])
- Good AI use requires discernment and judgment, and discernment/judgment come only from real experience. ([[raw/Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md#12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails|Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md › 12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails]])
- Andrés's own judgment about what actually happened and what mattered should always outrank a fluent-sounding AI rewrite. ([[raw/Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md#12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails|Berkeley Haas Resume Standards.md › 12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails]])
