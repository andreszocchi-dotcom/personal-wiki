# Berkeley Haas Resume Writing Standards

Style guide layer for the Career & Resume system. Consult this before writing/evaluating bullets; don't reproduce from memory when this file is available.

## 1. Bullet anatomy: WHAT / HOW / IMPACT / WHY

- **WHAT** — the thing you did / the work object you made
- **HOW** — the capability or method used to make it
- **IMPACT** — the result/outcome
- **WHY** — the context: why did the WHAT matter, what problem was being solved

**Three out of four is great.** Not every bullet needs all four; forcing all four every time produces bloated, run-on bullets.

Progression example (retail financial modeling):
1. WHAT only: "Developed financial models and scenarios for opening retail stores."
2. WHAT+HOW: "...by analyzing local demographics and transportation patterns."
3. WHAT+HOW+WHY: "...for the company's first foray into retail."
4. WHAT+HOW+IMPACT: "Adjusted financial models...resulting in +25% sales/sqft than projected." (this is the strongest — leads with an action verb, ends with quantified impact)

## 2. Emphasis order is a deliberate lever, not fixed

The same accomplishment can be resequenced to foreground whatever the target audience cares about most:
- **What+How+Why+Impact** — emphasizes the deliverable and process
- **Impact+How+What+Why** — leads with the number, for audiences that scan for metrics first (e.g., quant/finance readers)
- **How+Impact+What+Why** — emphasizes methodology/rigor (good for analytical/technical roles)
- **Why+What+How+Impact** — emphasizes strategic context and motivation (good when the "why" is the differentiator, e.g. a pivot story)

When tailoring the same bullet across target companies, resequencing (not just word-swapping) is the tool — confirmed by the pattern across Andrés's own tailored resumes (e.g., Deloitte bullets reordered/re-verbed between the Amazon, Adobe, Microsoft PMM, and Salesforce BVS versions while facts stay constant).

## 3. Average / Good / Great ladder

- *Average*: "Responsible for managing a team of sales representatives and meeting monthly targets." (pure responsibility, no ownership, no number)
- *Good*: "Led a team of 10 sales representatives, setting and achieving monthly sales targets, resulting in consistent revenue growth." (ownership + vague impact)
- *Great*: "Spearheaded a high-performing team of 10 sales representatives, driving a 20% increase in monthly revenue by implementing targeted sales strategies and conducting weekly coaching sessions." (ownership + specific mechanism + quantified impact)

Diagnostic questions for a flat bullet: What specifically did you do? What method/lever did you use? What changed as a result, numerically if possible?

## 4. Readability / conciseness test

Read the bullet aloud. If you stumble reading your own bullet, or a listener can't parse it on one read, it's too dense or too jargon-heavy. Apply this before finalizing any bullet, not just once at the end.

## 5. Quality control checklist (run on every bullet before it ships)

- [ ] **House rule (added per Andrés's feedback, 2026-08-24): no em dash used as a rhetorical connector inside a sentence** (the "X - which then did Y" pattern reads as AI-generated). Restructure with a period, semicolon, colon, or parentheses instead. Plain hyphens for dates, ranges, or name/date separators (e.g. "2020-2025," "Company Name - May 2027") are normal resume formatting and are NOT affected by this rule.
- [ ] **No litotes** (understatement via negation - "not uncommon," "no small feat"); state things directly.
- [ ] Is everything in the bullet literally true?
- [ ] Does it convey WHAT + HOW + IMPACT (at minimum two of the four elements)?
- [ ] Too many adjectives/adverbs? (cut them — nouns and verbs carry the weight)
- [ ] Too much jargon for the target reader?
- [ ] Do 3-letter acronyms match what *this* audience will recognize? (an acronym normal inside Deloitte/Workday may be opaque to a Google recruiter)
- [ ] Read it out loud — does it flow?
- [ ] Have someone else read it back to you — did they understand it the way you intended?

## 6. Formatting / structure choices

### Chronological format
- Classic, default choice
- Good for single pivots and adjacent pivots (PM→PMM, engineering→PM, consulting→BizOps/Strategy)
- Shows progression within one or two organizations
- Favored by traditional industries (finance, sports/entertainment, real estate shown in examples)

### Functional format
- Groups bullets by *skill category* (e.g., "Strategy and Analysis," "Systems Design," "Project Management," "Training and Development") across multiple employers, with a compressed title block up top listing org/title/dates
- Use for: big pivots, unfamiliar target industries, when the whole career has been in one narrow industry/vertical and needs to be translated into transferable skills
- Trade-off: obscures the chronological narrative in exchange for foregrounding transferable capability — don't use by default if chronological already tells the story cleanly

**For Andrés:** current resumes are chronological, which is correct — Deloitte→Haas MBA→ServiceNow PM intern is a legible, adjacent-ish pivot (consulting/tech-transformation → product management), not a "big pivot" requiring functional format.

### Company one-line descriptors
Directly under the company name, add an italicized one-liner that gives the recruiter unstated context. Choose the attribute from these buckets that's most *captivating to the current target audience* — don't just default to "scale":
- **Scale + industry**: "Latin America's leading e-commerce technology company, present in 18 countries, with net revenues of US$1.9bn"
- **Context** (for lesser-known / family / small orgs): "Family construction company founded in 1976; 37+ residential buildings with ~100 employees"
- **Industry credibility markers**: "A global CPG Certified B-corp (Avon, Natura, The Body Shop, and Aesop) with net revenues of US$7.5 billion"
- **Entrepreneurship/category-creation**: "The biggest taxi-and-rideshare company in Br and the first tech unicorn, with an annual revenue of over US$7M"

**For Andrés:** none of his current resumes use one-line company descriptors under "Deloitte Consulting LLP" or "ServiceNow." Deloitte and ServiceNow are both globally recognized brands so a descriptor may be low-value there — but "D2International – Fundación Juanfe" *does* get one ("Latin American non-profit focused on improving health, education, and employability of young mothers"), correctly, since that org is not a recognizable brand to a US recruiter. This pattern is already being applied correctly and should be preserved.

### Title vs. (Role) translation
Format: `"official title" (US-equivalent role)`. Use when the literal title won't be understood by a US recruiter/hiring manager, e.g. translating org-specific or non-US titles into the closest US-market equivalent:
- Corporate BD → Business Development Manager | Non-profit → Development Officer | Startup/Tech → Partnerships Manager | Real Estate → Acquisitions Manager | Finance → Client Relationship Manager
- Corporate Marketing → Marketing Manager | Startup/Tech → Growth Manager | Entertainment/Media → Promotions Manager | Retail → Brand Manager | Non-profit → Communications Manager

**For Andrés:** not currently applicable — "Senior Consultant / Consultant / Analyst" at Deloitte and "Senior Inbound Product Manager Intern" at ServiceNow are both already standard, legible US titles. Flag only if a future international or client-side title needs translating.

### Cautionary tales (avoid)
- Reference/security/background checks *will* verify title, dates, and scope — never inflate beyond what's defensible
- Touching a product ≠ "Product Manager" — don't claim a title/function you didn't actually hold
- Keyword stuffing — ATS does not auto-reject; it stores. A human is the real audience (see §7). Stuffed keywords read as inauthentic to that human.

## 7. ATS reality check
- ATS does not automatically accept/reject applications — it ingests and stores the resume.
- ATS platforms have used ML/AI-style matching since the 1990s; most of their scoring/parsing features are not actually used by the recruiters at a given company.
- **Bottom line: a human being is the target audience.** Optimize for the human reader first, keyword compatibility second — this directly matches Career & Resume LLM Core Instructions §15.

## 8. Beyond keywords — the real levers of tailoring
1. **Selecting experiences** — knowing the audience, choosing which projects/roles to surface at all
2. **Order of bullets** — most important/relevant first within each role
3. **Emphasis** — reweighting WHAT/HOW/WHY/IMPACT toward what a given pivot needs to prove (see §2)

Goal: make it easy for a recruiter/hiring manager to find what they're looking for. This is the same "signal per line" principle as Core Instructions §22 Phase 4.

## 9. Education section — standard optional fields
GPA, GMAT, awards, activities, applied-innovation coursework (e.g., Haas's Applied Innovation courses). Include selectively — only when they add signal (e.g., a strong GMAT/GPA, a scholarship, a directly relevant elective).

## 10. "Additional" section
Should read as *specific and memorable*, not generic filler. Contrast:
- Generic: "Skills: Product Management, Data Analysis, Agile..." — low signal, easy to skip
- Specific/memorable: "3x director & choreographer for Sweetgreen's corporate HQ team in fiercely competitive annual Talent Show; finally awarded the highly-coveted WIN last year" / "Amateur backyard farmer (guess what I'm growing), optimistic AI futurist, and washed-up distance runner (former D1 athlete)"

The specific version invites a follow-up question in an interview — that's the test for whether an "Additional" line is doing its job. Andrés's current Additional section ("Hiked in the Smokies, Andes, and Rockies | FC Barcelona and River Plate fan | Former Wedding Officiant") mostly clears this bar (Wedding Officiant is a great hook) but "FC Barcelona and River Plate fan" is closer to generic — worth testing whether there's a more specific story there (see open questions in Career Inventory).

## 11. Skill/category headings reference (Haas/CMG list)
When a resume needs a named skills/summary category header (functional-format section headers, or a capabilities line), pull from this bank rather than inventing generic ones. Organized by theme — pick the phrase that best matches what the bullets underneath actually demonstrate:

- **Leadership**: Leadership and Business Development · Leadership and Key Accomplishments · Collaborative Leadership · Leadership and Operations · Leadership & Organizational Impact · Leadership & Strategy
- **Strategy**: Strategic Planning · Strategy & Analysis · Strategy & Process Improvement · Organizational Strategy · Strategic Business Planning · Business Strategy
- **Organization/Implementation**: Project Management & Execution · Project Execution & Delivery · Project Management · Project & People Management
- **Relationship/Communication**: Cross-functional Teamwork · Cross-functional Team Leadership · Communication & Relationship Building · Persuasion & Influence · Influence Without Authority · Relationship Management · Client Relations
- **Change Management**: Organizational Transformation · Business Infrastructure and Development
- **Expertise**: Health IT Industry Expertise · Tech Systems Implementation & Optimization · Process Improvement · Operational Excellence · Contracts & Negotiations
- **Analysis**: Analysis & Problem Solving · Analytics · Quantitative and Qualitative Analysis · Financial Analysis and Valuation · Data Driven Decision Making · Financial Modeling
- **Creative**: Innovation and Strategy · Program Development & Design · Product Design and Development · Product Development · Innovation Leadership · Process Innovation
- **Marketing**: Marketing & Business Development · Sales & Business Development · Market Research & Competitive Analysis · Sales & Marketing
- **Business Functions**: Finance · Operations · New Product Development · Sales/Sales Management · Marketing · Engineering
- **Other**: "Be Creative" — Results Orientation, Initiative & Problem Solving is the given example of a bespoke category

Andrés's current resumes already use functional-style sub-headers within the Deloitte block ("Product Implementation & Execution," "Cross-Functional Team Leadership," "Strategic Vision & Client Engagement") — this is consistent with the bank above and should be reused/adapted per company rather than reinvented each time.

## 12. Generative AI use — principles and guardrails
1. GenAI feeling "brilliant" on a first draft usually just means you were inexperienced at that specific task — not a signal the output is actually great.
2. GenAI (LLMs), by construction, converges to an *average* (or below-average) output. Average is not the goal.
3. Good AI use requires discernment and judgment, and discernment/judgment come only from real experience. **Experience matters** — meaning: Andrés's own judgment about what actually happened and what mattered should always outrank a fluent-sounding AI rewrite.

**Sanctioned uses of GenAI**: rewrite bullets to generate *choices* (not a single final answer) · act as an editor to flag what's missing (WHAT/HOW/IMPACT/WHY gaps) · check/improve readability · analyze a job description to surface what's being emphasized.

This directly reinforces Core Instructions §2 ("do not rewrite a mediocre bullet until you've determined whether the story is mediocre or underexplored") and §11 (willingness to disagree) — the workshop's own guidance is that fluent AI output without underlying discernment produces average work, so this system should keep interviewing for real substance before polishing prose.
